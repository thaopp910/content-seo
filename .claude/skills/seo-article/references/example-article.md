# How to Use Laptop as Monitor: Easy Ways to Turn Your Laptop Into a Second Screen

Extra screen space makes work, study, and gaming easier. However, you do not need to buy a new monitor. This guide explains how to use laptop as monitor setups with Windows, Mac, and game consoles. You will also find quick fixes for common problems.

## What to Prepare Before Connecting a Laptop Display?

A little preparation saves a lot of troubleshooting later. Before you use a laptop as a monitor, check these basics:

- **Operating system:** Windows 10 or 11 for Miracast, or macOS Monterey and later for AirPlay.
- **Network:** Both devices on the same Wi-Fi network for wireless methods.
- **Ports:** A free USB 3.0 port if you plan to use a capture card.
- **Drivers:** Updated graphics and Wi-Fi drivers to reduce dropouts.

One detail surprises many users. Most laptop HDMI ports only send video out. As a result, an HDMI cable from a PC to a laptop usually shows nothing.

## How to Use Laptop as Monitor?

If you are wondering how to use a laptop as a monitor, there are four practical options. The table below compares them quickly.

| Method | Best for | Cable needed | Lag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Miracast | Windows to Windows | No | Medium |
| Capture card | Consoles and any HDMI source | Yes | Low |
| Third-party apps | Mixed systems | Optional | Low to medium |
| AirPlay or Sidecar | Apple users | No | Low |

Now, let's look at each method in practice.

### Method 1: Connect Wirelessly Using Windows Miracast

[Miracast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miracast) is a wireless display standard built into most Windows laptops. It needs no extra hardware.

1. On the receiving laptop, open **Settings > System > Projecting to this PC**.
2. Install the optional Wireless Display feature if asked.
3. On the main PC, press **Windows + K** and select the laptop.
4. Press **Windows + P** and choose Extend.

This is the simplest answer to how to use laptop as second monitor on Windows. Still, expect a slight delay during fast games.

### Method 2: Use a Video Capture Card for Direct HDMI Input

A capture card gives your laptop a real HDMI input over USB.

- Connect the HDMI source to the card.
- Plug the card into a USB 3.0 port.
- Open OBS Studio or the Windows Camera app.

Basic cards cost around $15 to $30, while Elgato models offer lower latency.

### Method 3: Stream Displays via Third-Party Applications

When devices run different systems, apps fill the gap. Duet Display works across Windows, macOS, iPad, and Android. Meanwhile, Deskreen lets any web browser act as an extra screen.

### Method 4: Use AirPlay or Sidecar for Apple Devices

Apple users already have built-in tools. On macOS Monterey or later, turn on AirPlay Receiver in **System Settings > General > AirDrop & Handoff**. Another Mac can then extend its screen to your MacBook. Similarly, iPad owners can use [Sidecar](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102597) as a second Mac display.

## How Do Players Display Console Games on a Laptop Screen?

Gamers often want to use a laptop as a monitor when the TV is busy. Since laptop HDMI ports do not accept input, two workarounds help:

- **Capture card:** Connect a PS5, Xbox, or Nintendo Switch to the card, then open OBS Studio for a sharp, low-lag image.
- **Remote Play apps:** Install PS Remote Play or the Xbox app. The console then streams games over your home network.

For competitive titles, choose a capture card. For casual play, Remote Play is enough.

## What Common Mistakes Block External Screen Connections?

When a laptop as monitor setup fails, small oversights are usually the cause. Watch for these mistakes:

- Plugging a PC into the laptop's HDMI output port.
- Using a USB 2.0 port for a capture card.
- Connecting one device to a guest network.
- Skipping the Wireless Display feature on Windows.
- Leaving a VPN or strict firewall active.

Fortunately, each one takes a minute to correct.

## How Can Users Fix Display Lag and Black Screen Issues?

Even a correct setup can show lag or a blank screen. This table matches each problem with a fix.

| Problem | Likely cause | Quick fix |
|---|---|---|
| Black screen from PS5 | HDCP enabled | Turn off HDCP in PS5 settings |
| Black screen with Miracast | Outdated drivers | Update drivers, then reconnect |
| Noticeable lag | Weak Wi-Fi signal | Use 5 GHz or move closer to the router |
| Stuttering video | Resolution too high | Lower the output to 1080p |

If problems continue, restart both devices.

## FAQs

### Does every laptop work as a monitor?

Most laptops can work as a monitor through software or a capture card. However, very few accept direct HDMI input.

### Is it possible to connect a laptop to a PC with HDMI?

Yes, but not with a plain HDMI cable. Laptop HDMI ports only output video, so you need a USB capture card in between.

### How does a laptop act as a monitor without Wi-Fi?

A capture card needs no network. Duet Display can also run over USB. In addition, Miracast uses Wi-Fi Direct, so no router is required.

### Which method works best for PS5 or Xbox?

A USB 3.0 capture card gives the lowest lag. Disable HDCP on PS5 first. Otherwise, try PS Remote Play or the Xbox app.

## Conclusion

Turning a spare laptop into an extra screen is easier than it looks. Windows users can start with Miracast, while Apple fans have AirPlay and Sidecar. Gamers get the best results from a capture card. Now that you know how to use laptop as monitor setups, pick the method that fits your devices.
