Anime fans lost a big option when Crunchyroll ended its free tier on December 31, 2025. Fortunately, plenty of legal **free anime websites** still exist. This guide compares 10 of them, explains what each one does well, and shows how to stay safe. In addition, it covers how to find shows that are actually free where you live.

### Tubi

Tubi offers one of the largest free anime libraries. Titles such as Death Note, Naruto, and Hunter x Hunter often appear, with both subbed and dubbed versions. However, video quality can be lower than paid services. Choose Tubi when you want to binge a full series on demand.

### Pluto TV

Pluto TV works like cable, with live channels plus an on-demand section. However, its anime lineup changed a lot in early 2026. Several franchise channels were dropped, while a general Pluto TV Anime channel was added. As a result, it suits casual viewers who like to drop in.

### RetroCrush

RetroCrush focuses on anime from the 1970s to the 1990s. Hard-to-find gems like Devilman and Galaxy Express 999 live here. The free tier has ads and needs no account. Meanwhile, Premium costs $4.99 a month and unlocks uncensored titles.

### Plex

Plex has a free, ad-supported catalog next to its media server tools. Its anime picks lean toward movies and cult classics, such as Ghost in the Shell. Still, the catalog rotates often, so save titles you want to watch soon.

### Official YouTube Channels

Many studios upload full episodes for free. TMS Anime shares shows like Lupin the Third, and GundamInfo often posts Gundam series. In Asia, [Muse Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muse_Communication) and Ani-One Asia simulcast current-season episodes. Always confirm the uploader is the official owner.

### The Roku Channel

The Roku Channel carries the free Crunchyroll Channel, a 24/7 stream of English-dubbed hits. Shows include Psycho-Pass and Code Geass. However, you cannot pick episodes. It works best as background viewing or a way to sample new genres.

### Samsung TV Plus

Samsung TV Plus now hosts a wide anime lineup in the US. Channels include It's Anime, Crunchyroll, RetroCrush, Naruto, and Sailor Moon. Naruto and Sailor Moon stream dubbed in English. It runs on Samsung TVs and phones, plus the web.

### Bilibili (Bstation)

Bstation is Bilibili's international app, popular in Southeast Asia. It hosts more than 2,000 licensed anime titles. Most content is free with ads, while some episodes need a membership. In addition, the app lets you download episodes for offline viewing.

### Hoopla

Hoopla lets you stream with a public library card. There is no fee and no credit card. Its TV catalog includes anime alongside comics and audiobooks. However, libraries cap monthly borrows, so plan which series to start.

### Kanopy

Kanopy also uses your library card. It focuses on films, including award-winning anime movies. As a result, it suits viewers who prefer quality films over long series. Check whether your library supports it before signing up.

## What Should You Check Before Choosing a Free Anime Website?

Not every site fits every fan. A great catalog is useless if it is blocked in your country or lacks dubs. In addition, free anime websites differ a lot in video quality and app support.

Check these points first:

- **Licensing:** the site should be a known brand or an official studio channel.
- **Region:** many services work only in certain countries.
- **Language:** confirm subs or dubs per title, not per site.
- **Ads:** expect breaks, but not pop-ups or redirects.
- **Devices:** look for apps on your TV, phone, or console.
- **Catalog rotation:** licensed shows leave when rights expire.

Fans of classics should start with RetroCrush. Meanwhile, binge watchers will get more from Tubi. Smart TV owners can lean on Samsung TV Plus or The Roku Channel.

## Are Free Anime Websites Safe and Legal?

Legal free anime websites are safe because they pay for licenses and earn money from ads. The risk comes from pirate sites, which use stolen streams and aggressive ad networks.

| Sign | Legal site | Pirate site |
|---|---|---|
| Ads | Short, video-style breaks | Pop-ups and redirects |
| Downloads | Inside an official app | Fake "Download" buttons |
| Brand | Known company or studio | Changing domain names |
| New episodes | Delayed or regional | Every show, instantly |

If a site offers every new show worldwide for free, it is almost certainly unlicensed. As a result, stick to known websites to watch anime free and skip anything that asks you to install extensions.

## How Can You Find Anime That Is Free in Your Region?

Licensing is local, so a show free in Canada may be paid in India. As a result, the same free anime websites can show different catalogs by country. Fortunately, a few habits save time.

1. Search the title on JustWatch and filter by free services.
2. Open each free app you can access and search directly, since catalogs change monthly.
3. Check your library's website for Hoopla or Kanopy.
4. In Asia, follow Muse Asia and Ani-One Asia on YouTube.
5. Look for a channel's region note, since YouTube blocks some uploads by country.

Using a VPN to unlock other regions may break a service's terms. So, try local options first.

## FAQs

### Can I Watch New Anime for Free?

Sometimes. Muse Asia and Ani-One Asia simulcast new episodes in parts of Asia. Elsewhere, most new shows sit behind paid services. However, studios like GundamInfo occasionally post new series on YouTube.

### Do Free Anime Websites Have English Subtitles and Dubs?

Yes, but it varies by title. Tubi and RetroCrush carry both, while Crunchyroll's free channel leans toward dubs. Check the title page before you start.

### Is Crunchyroll Still Free to Watch?

No. Crunchyroll removed its free ad-supported tier on December 31, 2025. Now, the full library needs a paid plan. Still, its free 24/7 channel runs on The Roku Channel and Samsung TV Plus.

## Conclusion

The best **free anime websites** depend on what you watch and where you live. Tubi and Samsung TV Plus cover most US fans, while RetroCrush suits classic lovers. Meanwhile, Bstation and Muse Asia serve Asia. Stick to licensed services, and you can enjoy anime for free without risking your device.
